# EdTech News This Week
*Updated: June 22, 2026*

- 2026-06-19: [Norway Says AI Ain’t for Education](https://gizmodo.com/norway-says-ai-aint-for-education-2000774320) - Norway is pushing back against classroom tech overkill, announcing restrictions on AI tools and social media in schools to prioritize traditional learning methods and protect student development. [Gizmodo.com]
- 2026-06-18: [New Accreditor Seeks to Turn ‘Bean-Counting’ Process Into Outcomes-Focused One](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/governance/accreditation/2026/06/18/board-chair-new-accreditor-discusses-timeline-goals) - A new accreditor, the Commission for Public Higher Education, aims to shift accreditation from bureaucratic "bean-counting" to a focus on student outcomes and institutional missions, backed by six state systems. [Inside Higher Ed]
- 2026-06-18: [Loan Autopay Enrollees to Temporarily Get Lower Interest Rates](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2026/06/18/autopay-enrollees-temporarily-get-lower-interest-rates) - To incentivize on-time payments, the Education Department will temporarily double the autopay discount on federal student loans, lowering rates by 0.5 percentage points instead of 0.25 for the next two years starting July 1. [Inside Higher Ed]
- 2026-06-17: [5 Things We Did Wrong with Edtech](https://edsurge.com/news/5-things-we-did-wrong-with-edtech) - A veteran educator argues that edtech's perceived failures stem from poor implementation—like prioritizing flashy tools over pedagogy and inadequate teacher training—not the technology itself. [Edsurge.com]
- 2026-06-16: [Michael M. Crow](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/michael-m-crow/) - ASU President Michael Crow advocates for universities to better communicate scientific research by directly connecting it to public values and societal needs, moving beyond traditional academic silos. [Scientific American]
- 2026-06-15: [Research on physics education where physics education happens](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03289-1) - Physics education researchers argue that embedding their work directly within physics departments—not just schools of education—yields mutual benefits, improving teaching practices while grounding research in real classroom dynamics. [Nature.com]
